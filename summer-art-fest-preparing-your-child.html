Summer Art Fest: Preparing Your Child for Expression

March 1, 2024 By Art Department 4 Min Read

The Annual Udaaan School Summer Art Fest is approaching, focusing entirely on three-dimensional tactile expression. This year's core material? Natural earthen clay.

We are shifting away from traditional flat crayons and embracing the sensory-rich experience of pulling, tearing, pinching, and shaping dirt. Tactile art creates neural pathways completely separate from typical academic tasks.

1. Prepare for the Mess

First and foremost, parents must detach from the anxiety of clean clothes. Send your children in their designated 'Art Smocks' or old t-shirts. If a child fears reprimand for getting dirt on their clothing, their absolute creative freedom is severely inhibited.

2. Praise the Process, Not the Result

A child might hand you a gray, unidentifiable lump of clay and proudly declare it a 'Ferrari'. Do not correct them. Do not enforce realism. Say, "Tell me about how you made this!" rather than "What is this?" This actively validates their internal creative process rather than demanding adult-level production quality.

3. Extended Home Access

If your child shows deep interest in the festival, extend it. Purchase natural air-dry clay (not just synthetic play-dough) to keep at home. The resistance of real clay builds extraordinary hand strength needed later for precise handwriting control.

4. Event Details

The Art Fest takes place on the main courtyard lawn on Friday, April 14th. Parents are strongly encouraged to attend and, quite frankly, urged to get their hands dirty alongside the students.
